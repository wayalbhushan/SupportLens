> [!NOTE]
> **Key Takeaways & Cost-Asymmetry Observations:**
> 1. **Why Baseline 2 Loses to Baseline 1 on Raw Accuracy (47.5% vs 48.3%):** Baseline 2 was trained entirely on pseudo-labels generated by Baseline 1's keyword heuristic. A machine learning model cannot beat the heuristic that produced its own training signal when evaluated against clean ground truth the heuristic never saw. Baseline 2 is, at best, a smoothed approximation of the keyword rules with linear generalization — on an 8-class imbalanced classification problem, this ceiling matches the empirical result.
> 2. **Misleading Headline Metrics Case Study (75.0% Val vs 47.5% Test Accuracy):** The 27.5-point gap between validation accuracy on weak labels (75.0%) and golden set accuracy (47.5%) is a textbook case of inflated offline validation. Evaluating exclusively on weak-label splits would give an illusion of high performance, whereas holding out an independently curated golden set immediately uncovers the reality.
> 3. **Escalation Precision/Recall Trade-Off:** Baseline 1 predicts `escalate=True` unconditionally (100% recall, 22.5% precision on Stratum A). Baseline 2 applies the deterministic default policy to predicted intent: precision jumps from 22.5% to **70.6%**, eliminating almost 70% of unnecessary human escalations, but recall drops to **44.4%** due to missed intent classifications.
> 4. **Structural Failure Mode (Hypothesis 1 Confirmed): Regulatory & Legal Threat Suppression:** In the keyword dictionary, regulatory terms (`fcc`) were placed strictly under `other`. In golden set evaluation, messages threatening FCC/FTC/BBB filings or attorney retention (`GS-197`, `GS-200`) were classified as `other` and deterministically assigned `escalate=False`. Furthermore, technical defects with legal threats (`GS-185`, class-action threat over slow speeds) were classified as `connectivity_issue` and auto-handled (`escalate=False`). Baseline 2 achieved **0 / 3 (0.0% recall)** on legal-regulatory escalations.
> 5. **The Bar for Phase 4:** The Phase 4 LLM Agent must surpass **48.3% Stratum A accuracy** and **0.466 Macro-F1**, while achieving both high Escalation Recall (>85%) AND high Precision (>70%) simultaneously.

## 2. Baseline 1: Trivial Keyword Baseline

### Method
- **Algorithm:** Case-insensitive keyword matching from `src/keyword_dict.py` checked in fixed priority order: `account_access` → `appointment_installation` → `billing_dispute` → `tv_content_access` → `connectivity_issue` → `praise_thanks` → `complaint_no_request` → `other`. First match wins; unmatched messages predict `other`.
- **Escalation Rule:** Always predicts `escalate=True` for every incoming message (deliberate floor).
- **Priority Order Justification & Caveat:** Placing `account_access` ahead of `connectivity_issue` is strictly grounded in Tie-Break Rule 3 of LABELING_GUIDE.md v1.2 (credential/authentication failure is a hard blocker to technical troubleshooting). Placing `billing_dispute` ahead of connectivity issues is an engineering heuristic chosen for this baseline floor to capture financial disputes, rather than an explicit mandate of the guide.

## 3. Baseline 2: Simple Weak-Supervision Baseline

### Method
- **Training Pool:** 2,500 messages sampled from in-scope candidate pool (`random_state=42`) strictly excluding all 200 golden set items (asserted zero leakage).
- **Weak-Labeling:** Same keyword dictionary and priority order used in Baseline 1.
- **Architecture:** TF-IDF (5,000 max features, English stop words, 1-2 ngrams) + Multiclass Logistic Regression (`class_weight='balanced'`, `max_iter=1000`).
- **Escalation Rule:** Deterministic mapping from model's predicted intent per Section 4 of `LABELING_GUIDE.md` v1.2 (`billing_dispute`, `account_access`, `appointment_installation` → True; others → False).
